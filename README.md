One sentence, 147 characters, explaining the modern internet to someone who has just stepped out of the year 1800 — built for make.fun's "One Sentence for a Traveler from 1800" bounty ($2 in USDC).

> It is a road joining every house on earth, where a letter arrives before the ink dries and a book from any library can be read from your own chair.

## Why this sentence

The task allows no word coined after 1800, no list of features, just a picture a person of that year could hold in their head. The sentence is a single comparison — a road — and the road carries the two things the internet is mostly used for.

1. **It explains what the internet is and does.** A road joining every house on earth says connection; a letter arriving at once says instant communication; a book from any library read from your own chair says access to all knowledge. A traveler from 1800 grasps all three from things they already know.
2. **It builds only from 1800 knowledge.** Road, house, letter, ink, book, library, chair — every word existed in 1800. There is no telegraph, telephone, computer, website, screen, or email.
3. **It is true, taken literally.** Letters really do arrive in seconds, and books from libraries around the world really can be read from home. The wording stays careful on purpose: "a book from any library", not "any book", because not every book is online.
4. **Every word earns its place.** One comparison, two clauses, 147 of the 200 allowed characters. Nothing is filler and nothing is a crammed list of features.
5. **It leaves an image behind.** "A letter arrives before the ink dries" is the phrase a reader would still remember a week later.

## How to run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

That writes `out/entry.txt` — exactly the sentence above, with no trailing newline. `main.ts` uses only the Node standard library, writes deterministically, and refuses to write anything outside the 1–200 character limit or anything that is not exactly one sentence.
